# Theory of packet parsing: scope, models, and IR expressiveness

Questions: (1) Is there a consensus on what "packet parsing" involves vs. out-of-scope? (2) How can a packet-parsing IR be shown expressive enough — and not over-general? (3) What mathematical models of packet parsers exist?

Method: deep-research run (2026-07-29). 5 parallel threads — P4 formal semantics & equivalence; symbolic automata/transducers; data-dependent grammars & verified format languages; hardware parse-graph theory; langsec — each reading primary sources (papers, specs) and flagging unverified claims. Claims below cite primary sources; secondhand or gray-literature items are marked.

## Q1: Scope — two communities, two boundaries

**The dataplane community has a stable, explicit, operational definition, unchanged since 2010:**

- Kangaroo (INFOCOM 2010): "By parsing we mean the exploration of a few selected fields of a packet directed by a parse tree, as opposed to a scan of all payload bytes looking for a regular expression." [Kozanitis et al.](https://cseweb.ucsd.edu/~varghese/PAPERS/kangaroo.pdf)
- Gibb et al. (ANCS 2013): "Parsing is the process of identifying headers and extracting fields for processing by subsequent stages... Parsing is inherently sequential: each header is identified by the preceding header." Regex/L7 matching explicitly contrasted as a different design space. [Gibb et al.](https://yuba.stanford.edu/~nickm/papers/ancs48-gibb.pdf)
- RMT (SIGCOMM 2013): headers only — "General purpose payload processing is not our goal"; payload bypasses the pipeline and rejoins at the deparser; the 4Kb packet-header-vector is the hard bound on what "headers" can be. [Bosshart et al.](https://conferences.sigcomm.org/sigcomm/2013/papers/sigcomm/p99.pdf)
- PSA/TNA specs define payload *negatively*, as the residue: "the part that was not parsed by the ingress parser" ([PSA §7.5.1](https://p4lang.github.io/p4-spec/docs/PSA.pdf)); "the portion of the packet left after parsing" ([TNA](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/barefootnetworks/Open-Tofino/master/PUBLIC_Tofino-Native-Arch.pdf)).
- Reassembly is never even mentioned as a parser function in any dataplane spec examined — out of scope by total absence, not by explicit exclusion.

So the de facto dataplane consensus: **per-packet, header-prefix-only, parse-graph/FSM-directed, terminating, line-rate-bounded**; payload = unparsed residue.

**Langsec draws the boundary at full recognition instead:** a correct implementation is "defined by the decision problem of whether the byte string received... is a member of the protocol's language," decomposed into syntactic validity ("corresponds to the parser") vs. valid state transition ("the remainder of the implementation") ([Halting Problems, ;login: 2011](https://www.usenix.org/publications/login/december-2011-volume-36-number-6/halting-problems-network-stack-insecurity)); "Apply full recognition to inputs before processing them!" ([Occupy Babel](https://langsec.org/occupy/)). Payload formats are in scope; stopping at headers is a truncation.

**The contested middle** (where IR design decisions live): variable-length/TLV structures (in scope for both; where the hardware model strains), validation (P4 bolts on `verify`; langsec makes rejection the point), serialization (verified-format community treats parse/serialize as a pair with laws; dataplane deparsers are under-theorized), multi-packet context (out for dataplane, central for Zeek/Spicy).

Conclusion: no cross-community consensus; the dataplane boundary is a design choice with a physical rationale (bounded per-packet work at line rate), not a theorem. A property-based boundary — "parsing = the decidable, bounded subset of packet processing" — is sharper than either community's feature list and is consistent with both the dataplane quotes above and langsec's minimal-computational-power principle.

## Q3: The mathematical models (surveyed before Q2, since Q2's answer builds on them)

### Parse graphs and the hardware model

- **Parse graph** (Gibb, ANCS 2013): a DAG, vertices = header types, edges = sequential ordering; "the state machine that sequentially identifies the header sequence." Loops don't exist in the model — MPLS/VLAN stacks are unrolled by definition (Kangaroo: "unrolling recursive traversals... up to the specified limits," which "must be specified"). Split into header identification + parallel field extraction; implemented as TCAM+RAM state machine. [Gibb et al.](https://yuba.stanford.edu/~nickm/papers/ancs48-gibb.pdf)
- **Resource theory, such as it is:** Kangaroo's worst-case speed model (parsing speed = min over parse-tree paths of header-bits/cycles; worst case is short-header stacks, not min-size packets) plus an exact clustering DP, O(|E||V|d^k). Gibb: table minimization justified as NP-hard by citation to graph clustering (no reduction for the exact problem published — apparently still unclaimed); streaming DP optimal on trees only; empirical law: parser area ∝ total extracted bits. Recent: ParserHawk (SIGCOMM 2025) recasts parser compilation as CEGIS program synthesis over hardware resource models, giving SMT-backed infeasibility proofs where rule-based compilers falsely reject. [ParserHawk](https://people.cs.rutgers.edu/~sn624/papers/parserhawk-sigcomm25.pdf)

### The P4 abstract machine and its termination gap

- P4-16 parser = FSM with `start`/`accept`/`reject`, cursor, extract/lookahead/advance, select, header stacks, sub-parsers (recursion impossible by definition-before-use). Loops are legal; the linear-complexity claim is **conditional**: "provided some header is extracted on each cycle, the packet itself provides a bound" — the language does not enforce the condition. Targets may impose static or dynamic budgets and must reject to `error.ParserTimeout` on overrun ([P4-16 v1.2.5 §13, §1.1](https://p4.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/53/2024/10/P4-16-spec-v1.2.5.pdf)). Tofino ships exactly this: loop-counter-range and dual timeout parser errors ([TNA](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/barefootnetworks/Open-Tofino/master/PUBLIC_Tofino-Native-Arch.pdf)).
- Unrestricted P4 parsers are Turing-complete via a two-counter-machine encoding (parser states + arbitrary-width variables + no forced extraction), so termination is undecidable in general — argued in [p4-spec issue #46](https://github.com/p4lang/p4-spec/issues/46) (proof sketch in an issue thread, never published; this motivated the spec's budget language).
- Petr4 (POPL 2021) gives parsers an executable semantics, but its mechanized termination theorem is for the parser-free calculus only; parsers retain it only after unrolling ("The most significant omission from Core P4 is parsers"). [Petr4](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/~jnfoster/papers/petr4.pdf)

### P4 automata and decidable equivalence (Leapfrog)

- **The pivotal model.** Leapfrog (PLDI 2022) defines P4 automata: finite states, store = fixed-width bitvector headers, per-state straight-line ops (extract + assignments) then goto/select; **every state must extract ≥ 1 bit** (progress ⇒ termination). Because states, store, and buffers are all finite, "⟨C, δ, F⟩ is a DFA" — equivalence "is decidable, because S is finite and language equivalence of DFAs is decidable." Decidability is trivial; the configuration space is astronomical (≥10^38 for a toy MPLS parser), so the contribution is a *symbolic* bisimulation ("bisimulation with leaps") computed via weakest preconditions + SMT, metatheory in Coq. [Leapfrog](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.08762)
- Costs and limits: variable-length benchmark (2 optional fields) took 953 min / 405 GB; largest real benchmark ran out of memory at ~500 GB. Excluded from the model: header stacks, sub-parsers, lookahead, externs; extract sizes are static per state — **data-dependent cursor advance is encoded by branching lengths into control states, not modeled natively**. Coq proves soundness only (completeness/termination on paper; SMT solver in the TCB).
- Follow-ons: Octopus (BSc thesis, Leiden 2025, unrefereed) reimplements the algorithm outside Coq with certificates, replicating Leapfrog's results on a PC — decertification buys ~3 orders of magnitude ([thesis](https://theses.liacs.nl/pdf/2024-2025-LeenenJPJort.pdf)). HOL4P4 (OOPSLA 2024) mechanizes full P4-16 incl. externs/concurrency; Verifiable P4 (ITP 2023) + [arXiv:2412.19908](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19908) extend foundational verification to parsers/deparsers; Gauntlet (OSDI 2020) checked equivalence only for programs *without* parsers — Leapfrog remains the only certified parser-equivalence result. P4DDG (GPCE 2025) transpiles data-dependent grammars to P4 parsers (listing only; not read).

### Symbolic automata: where the decidability frontier actually is

The symbolic-automata literature maps the frontier precisely; three walls recur across every model ([CAV 2017 survey](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-319-63387-9_3), [CACM 2021](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3419404)):

1. **The predicate algebra must be decidable** — everything is "modulo theory" (SFA equivalence/minimization/closure all reduce to predicate satisfiability). [POPL 2012](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/2103656.2103674)
2. **Determinism/single-valuedness is required once outputs or registers appear** — single-valued SFT equivalence decidable, relational SFT equivalence undecidable; deterministic symbolic register automata equivalence decidable, nondeterministic undecidable ([SRA, CAV 2019](https://cseweb.ucsd.edu/~ldantoni/papers/cav19-sra.pdf)).
3. **Any relation between input symbols or arithmetic between registers is fatal** — extended SFAs (k-symbol lookahead guards) have undecidable equivalence even single-valued, except the Cartesian-guard fragment ([FMSD 2015](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10703-015-0233-4)); registers with linear arithmetic subsume two-counter machines ([LICS 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.03972)).

**No named class exactly matches "bytes + bounded bitvector registers + register-dependent cursor advance + hard step budget."** Each neighbor fails differently: SFA/SFT lack registers; ESFA lack persistent registers and lose decidability; SRA registers are equality-only (no "advance by v"); SST/CRA registers are write-only outputs that can't steer control. Such a machine is finite-state by finiteness of its configuration space — strictly weaker than every undecidable model above, which is exactly *why* it stays decidable. Leapfrog is the closest concrete instantiation; the abstract class (finite-unfolding symbolic machines with bitvector-arithmetic guards) is unnamed and unstudied. Bounded-variable EFSMs are the older protocol-modeling analogue: finite in principle, state-explosion in practice — the same trade Leapfrog fights symbolically.

### Grammars and format calculi

- **DDC / "The next 700 data description languages"** (POPL 2006): dependent-type calculus giving the first formal semantics for data-description languages; each type denotes a representation type, a parse-descriptor type, and a parsing function. Theorems: type correctness; error correlation (PD reports zero errors ⇒ representation is error-free). Encodes PADS, PacketTypes, DataScript in one calculus — still the only unifying semantic comparison of DDLs. No serializer/round-trip theorem. [DDC](https://www.cs.princeton.edu/~dpw/papers/700popl06.pdf)
- **Data-dependent grammars / Yakker** (POPL 2010): CFGs + variable binding + arbitrary constraints + parameterized nonterminals. Closure theorem (union/concatenation/Kleene star — which PEG-style prioritized choice breaks); grammar→automaton translation and Earley-style algorithm proven correct. No polynomial bound in the data-dependent case. [DDG](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/1706299.1706347)
- **Calc-regular languages** (LangSec/SPW 2017): the formal class for "regular + length fields." Theorem 2: netstrings are **not context-free** (pumping lemma), with the finiteness caveat stated precisely: bounded-length instances are finite hence regular — the class matters for succinctness, not membership. Calc-regular = prefix-free regexes + length-prefix and count-prefix operators ≡ FSMs with accumulators; parsing ≈ regular cost. Strictly incomparable with (D)CFL: length-prefixing and nesting are *orthogonal* escapes from regularity. Not closed under union; nested length-prefixing (MessagePack-style) is outside — "calc-context-free" sketched as future work. [Grosch, Koenig, Lucks](http://spw17.langsec.org/papers/grosch-taming-length-fiels.pdf)
- **Interval parsing grammars** (PLDI 2023): attach input intervals to nonterminals — handles random access/non-contiguous formats (ZIP end-scan, PDF xref) that left-to-right DDGs, PEGs, and CFGs provably cannot specify; static termination check (proven) and O(n²) parsing; formally exhibits Kaitai `seek` nontermination. [IPG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04859). Daedalus (PLDI 2024) is the systems sibling. Kaitai and Spicy/binpac have **no published formal characterization at all** (verified absence).

### Verified round-trip parsers

- **EverParse** (USENIX Security 2019): the precise vocabulary — correct (p(s(m)) = m), exact, **non-malleable** (p(x) = p(y) ⇒ x = y ∨ ⊥; each message has at most one wire image), complete; "secure parser" = non-malleable + complete, whose inverse *is* the serializer. Format class: TLV with concatenated-field dependencies only (TLS Heartbeat's `padding_length` inexpressible; untagged unions get only conditional guarantees). [EverParse](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity19/presentation/delignat-lavaud). 3d (PLDI 2022) adds double-fetch freedom and ships in the Windows kernel Hyper-V stack — 100+ formats, the strongest production claim in this literature. [3d](https://fstar-lang.org/papers/EverParse3D.pdf)
- **Narcissus** (ICFP 2019): formats as nondeterministic stateful *relations* (S × Σ × T × Σ); encoders refine the format, decoders are sound-and-strict inverses modulo a state relation; round-trip theorems both ways; corollary: ambiguous formats have no correct decoder. Derived Ethernet/ARP/IPv4/TCP/UDP in Coq, swapped into MirageOS. [Narcissus](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3341686)
- Nail (OSDI 2014): parser/generator pairs, but the "semantic bijection" is unproven and transformations are an unverified escape hatch. Rendel & Ostermann's invertible syntax descriptions (Haskell 2010) is the algebraic ancestor (partial isomorphisms; no mechanization).

### Langsec: theorems vs. doctrine

The corpus separates cleanly. **Actual theorems:** the imported classics (CFG equivalence/containment/ambiguity undecidable; DPDA equivalence decidable per Sénizergues); PKCS#1 is context-sensitive (LBA construction, [Systems Journal 2013](https://www.cs.dartmouth.edu/~sergey/langsec/papers/sassaman-jsys7-3.pdf)); the calc-regular results above. **Doctrine argued from them:** full recognition before processing, minimal computational power, the Postel critique, "parser computational equivalence" as a composition requirement. Two recurring formal soft spots, both documented in the primary texts: (a) the papers state "all unambiguous CFGs are deterministic" — standard theory has the strict inclusion reversed (DCFL ⊊ unambiguous CFL; even palindromes separate them), and the conflation recurs across the corpus; (b) the slide from "undecidable in general" to "uncheckable in any specific case." Two later results partially retract (b): *structural* equivalence of CFGs is decidable even nondeterministically, via parenthesized grammars — directly relevant to parser-differential detection ([Ali & Smith, LangSec 2023](https://langsec.org/spw23/papers/Ali_LangSec23.pdf)); and bounded length fields are finite hence regular, so the meaningful measure is succinctness, not language class — the recognizing automaton grows exponentially in field width ([Ganty, Köpf, Valero](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.07198); also conceded in [Seven Turrets](https://langsec.org/papers/langsec-cwes-secdev2016.pdf) via the LBA passage). Parser differentials: canonical exploits are [PKI Layer Cake](https://ifca.ai/pub/fc10/13_127.pdf) (FC 2010) and Android master key (CVE-2013-4787). Scope critique from inside the community: injection flaws exploit *correct* parsing and are unparsing problems, untouched by full recognition ([Poll, 2018](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/login/articles/login_winter18_05_poll.pdf)).

## Q2: IR adequacy — the synthesized answer

"Expressive enough for all possible packet parsers" is ill-posed: unrestricted P4 is already Turing-complete (issue #46), and a Turing-complete IR forfeits every analysis that makes an IR worth having. The literature supports reframing adequacy as three separable arguments:

1. **Upper bound (a model, not a feature list).** Fix the machine class the IR denotes and prove IR ≡ model. The evidence says the right class is Leapfrog-shaped: finite states + fixed-width bitvector store + a progress/budget condition ⇒ the whole machine is a finite automaton, and equivalence is decidable *by construction*. A per-packet state-entry budget is strictly easier than Leapfrog's extract-per-state condition (unroll into ≤ B copies → DAG); no paper states the budgeted version verbatim — that exact statement is unclaimed ground.
2. **Lower bound (corpus necessity).** The known hard cases that separate real classes, extracted from the literature: length-prefix fields (calc-regular, non-CF), counted repetition, nested length-prefixing (beyond calc-regular), TLV walks (P4's folklore-hard case; Leapfrog's 405 GB case), header stacks (excluded from P4 automata), non-left-to-right access (IPG's separating examples — arguably out of scope for streaming packet parsers, and a good place to *draw* the boundary), non-concatenated-field dependencies (EverParse's exclusion). Each is a witness a feature either covers or deliberately excludes.
3. **Anti-genericity (minimality + decidability preservation).** Independence: each construct not macro-expressible via the others (sugar goes in frontends). Witness rule: no construct without a corpus protocol needing it. Frontier rule: no construct that crosses the three walls from the symbolic-automata literature — undecidable predicate theories, nondeterminism/multi-valuedness, or arithmetic relations between unbounded registers. Inside a hard budget, the third wall softens (everything is finite anyway), but succinctness of analysis is the real cost being spent: Leapfrog's numbers are the empirical price list.

Litmus test from the literature: parser-equivalence checking is the one analysis that is simultaneously decidable for the right class (Leapfrog), the weapon against the dominant vulnerability pattern (parser differentials), and the portability guarantee (translation validation of parser compilers — Leapfrog validated a third-party hardware parser compiler). An IR whose every feature keeps equivalence checking practical is expressive enough and not too generic, in the only sense that is falsifiable.

## Open ground surfaced by this survey

- No named/studied automata class for "finite-unfolding symbolic machines with bitvector-arithmetic guards" — the class packet parsers actually live in.
- Native data-dependent cursor advance in equivalence checking (Leapfrog unfolds lengths into control states; the blowup is the 405 GB).
- "Calc-context-free" (nested length-prefixing) named but never developed.
- NP-hardness of the exact TCAM parse-table minimization problem: cited by analogy, never proven.
- Compositional parser equivalence (named as future work in Leapfrog; no successor as of 2026-07).
- The hardware literature (parse graphs → TCAM) and the verified-format literature (dependent formats → round-trip laws) barely cite each other; deparsing/serialization is where the gap is widest — dataplane deparsers have no round-trip theory, while EverParse/Narcissus have the theorems but not the streaming/line-rate model.

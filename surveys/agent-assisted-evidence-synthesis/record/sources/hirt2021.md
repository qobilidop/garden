# Agreement in Risk of Bias Assessment Between RobotReviewer and Human Reviewers: An Evaluation Study on Randomised Controlled Trials in Nursing‐Related Cochrane Reviews

## Evidence
- ML system: RobotReviewer (same semi-automated RoB tool as arno2022), used here as the "index test" against human Cochrane-review RoB judgments as the "reference test" — a retrospective diagnostic-accuracy design, not an RCT (contrast with arno2022's noninferiority RCT of the same tool).
- RoB instrument: Cochrane risk of bias version 2011 (RoB 1), restricted to three domains — random sequence generation, allocation concealment, and blinding (of personnel/participants and of outcome assessors).
- Corpus: convenience sample of 190 RCTs published 1958-2016, drawn from 23 Cochrane reviews (published 2000-2018) whose title contained "nurs*" — i.e., nursing-related Cochrane reviews specifically, not a general-medicine sample. Sample size per domain varied because of missing RoB assessments in either the Cochrane reviews or RobotReviewer's output.
- Reviewer process: "two independent research teams performed and double-checked data extraction and analysis" — this describes independence of the *human data-extraction teams* auditing the comparison, not independent replicate runs of RobotReviewer itself (RobotReviewer's own judgments are treated as a single index-test output per trial/domain).
- Metrics reported: sensitivity, specificity, ROC curve and AUC, predictive values, observed percent agreement, and Cohen's kappa (with CIs where applicable) — a diagnostic-accuracy metric suite layered on top of raw/kappa agreement, distinct from arno2022's single-accuracy-percentage framing.
- Headline numbers: sensitivity ranged 0.44-0.88 and specificity 0.48-0.95 across domains. Positive predictive value was highest for allocation concealment (0.79) and lowest for blinding of outcome assessors (0.25). Cohen's kappa: randomization 0.52 (moderate), allocation concealment 0.60 (moderate), blinding of personnel/participants 0.43 (moderate), blinding of outcome assessors 0.04 (slight) — a large domain-to-domain spread, with outcome-assessor blinding essentially at chance-level agreement despite RobotReviewer producing confident predictions there.
- Authors' framing: RobotReviewer showed "a moderate degree of agreement with human reviewers for randomization and allocation concealment, and an adequate sensitivity for detecting low risk of selection bias," but recommend "human reviewers should supervise the semi-automated assessment process" rather than treating RobotReviewer output as authoritative.

## Bearing on RQs
- **Reliability measured heterogeneously / class imbalance:** directly illustrates the survey's heterogeneous-metrics concern within a single paper: the same RobotReviewer-vs-human comparison yields moderate kappa (0.43-0.60) for three domains but slight kappa (0.04) for a fourth, and the paper additionally reports sensitivity/specificity/AUC/PPV alongside kappa — a diagnostic-accuracy framing not used by arno2022's single-accuracy-number framing or by rose2025's planned single overall-kappa framing. It is a concrete case where per-domain kappa exposes a near-chance-agreement failure mode (blinding-of-assessors, PPV 0.25) that an aggregate accuracy percentage (as in arno2022, 88.8% vs 90.2% overall) would likely mask — a direct evidentiary example, not just an assertion, of metric choice changing the reliability picture.
- **Appraisal stage / RCT-grade evidence:** this is a second RobotReviewer evaluation at the appraisal stage, but — unlike arno2022 — it is a retrospective diagnostic-accuracy study against archival Cochrane judgments, not an RCT with prospective reviewer randomization. It does not add to the appraisal stage's RCT-grade evidence base; it corroborates arno2022's general finding (RobotReviewer usable but imperfect and needing supervision) from a different design and a narrower, nursing-specific corpus, and shows the agreement picture is domain-dependent rather than uniform.
- **Independence of agent reviewers:** does not bear on this question. RobotReviewer here produces one index-test judgment per trial/domain (not duplicate/parallel runs), and the "two independent research teams" language describes human audit of the extraction process, not independence between automated assessors.

## Evidence limits
- Abstract-only: full text is paywalled at the publisher (Wiley/Sigma Theta Tau host, 402 Payment Required on direct fetch); Europe PMC flags the record `isOpenAccess:N`. No independent OA search beyond Europe PMC's own access flag was performed (unlike arno2022's broader Unpaywall/Semantic Scholar/institutional-repository sweep) — a closed PDF may exist through routes not checked here.
- All facts above come from the PubMed/Europe PMC abstract only: no access to full methods (e.g., exact statistical procedures beyond the named metrics, how missing-domain sample sizes were handled per analysis, or discussion of why outcome-assessor blinding underperforms).
- Convenience sample restricted to "nurs*"-titled Cochrane reviews (190 RCTs, 1958-2016) — a narrow, single-specialty corpus; the authors' own framing ("first evaluation... in nursing-related Cochrane reviews") flags this as a novel population, not a general-medicine result, and it should not be pooled with arno2022's general "real-world" Cochrane sample without noting the difference.
- RobotReviewer, like in arno2022, is a narrow pre-LLM ML system (trained/fine-tuned classifiers), not an LLM agent; relevance to this survey's agentic core is as a pre-LLM baseline/comparator, not a directly comparable method.

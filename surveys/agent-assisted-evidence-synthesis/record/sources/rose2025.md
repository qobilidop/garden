# Using a large language model (ChatGPT) to assess risk of bias in randomized controlled trials of medical interventions: protocol for a pilot study of interrater agreement with human reviewers

## Evidence
- **This is a study protocol, not a completed evaluation.** The full published text (BMC Med Res Methodol, July 2025) is written entirely in future tense ("we will randomly select...", "we will estimate...") and reports zero empirical numbers — no agreement percentage, no kappa value, no discordance count. The "Results" section of the paper itself is a plan for what will be reported, not a report of findings: "we will report observed agreement with an exact 95% confidence interval, expected agreement under random assessment, Cohen's κ, and a p-value testing the null hypothesis of no difference in agreement."
- Planned design: 100 individually- or cluster-randomized, parallel, two-arm medical-intervention trials will be drawn from recent Cochrane systematic reviews (RoB assessed with the RoB1 or RoB2 family of tools; reviews covering emergency conditions such as COVID-19, and public-health/welfare interventions, are excluded). 25 trials will be used only for prompt engineering (informal, not part of the analysis); the remaining 75 are earmarked for the interrater-agreement estimate, planned to give "at least 90% power at the 95% significance level."
- Planned reference standard: "human consensus-based overall RoB assessments of trials obtained from Cochrane SRs," defined as "at least one reviewer assessed RoB and at least two reviewers agreed on the final assessment" — a consensus/majority construction from the original Cochrane review teams, not a fresh independent human re-assessment commissioned for this study.
- Planned LLM protocol: ChatGPT from "the GPT-3.5 series" (both GPT-3.5 and GPT-4 to be tried during prompt engineering, final choice unspecified in the protocol); assessment is of **overall** RoB only (three categories: high / unclear / low), not per-domain — a coarser grain than hirt2021's four-domain RobotReviewer comparison. If ChatGPT's reply does not clearly land in one of the three categories, "one non-prespecified follow-on question" will be asked.
- Independence construction (directly relevant to the survey's open question): "Two different people will each independently assess each trial using ChatGPT (duplicate assessments)." Here, independence between the two ChatGPT-mediated assessments is defined operationally as *different human operators driving separate ChatGPT sessions*, not as any property of the model itself (e.g., different seeds, different context windows, or architecturally separate agents). The protocol does not address session/conversation-history leakage, temperature/sampling settings, or whether the two operators could see each other's prompts or outputs.
- Stated limitations (verbatim/near-verbatim from the paper): results "may not generalize beyond the population defined by inclusion criteria..., trial primary outcomes (which usually assess efficacy rather than safety), trials whose methods text can easily be extracted electronically, or to future versions of ChatGPT or other LLMs"; RoB1's "Uncertain RoB" and RoB2's "Some concerns" categories are treated as substantially overlapping without being identical; the primary outcome targets trial-level (not outcome-level) RoB, which the authors themselves flag as a limitation since a trial's RoB can differ by outcome.

## Bearing on RQs
- **Appraisal is the map's thin end / RCT-grade evidence:** does not add an appraisal-stage empirical result — v1's finding that arno2022 is "essentially one RCT-grade automation study" at the appraisal stage stands unchanged by this work, because this paper is upstream of any results: it is a pre-registered design, not a completed pilot. It is relevant only as a forward pointer — a well-specified, adequately powered (75-trial, 90%-power) design for a future LLM-vs-human kappa study — and as a methodological contribution (a concrete power/sample-size justification and consensus-reference-standard definition that a later completed study, or this survey's next update, should look for).
- **Reliability measured heterogeneously:** the protocol commits in advance to a single overall-agreement metric (Cohen's κ on a 3-category overall judgment) plus observed/expected agreement and a significance test — a narrower, single-endpoint design than hirt2021's per-domain kappa+sensitivity+specificity+AUC suite or arno2022's raw-accuracy framing. Because it assesses **overall** RoB rather than per-domain, it cannot by construction surface the kind of domain-specific near-chance agreement hirt2021 found for blinding-of-assessors (kappa 0.04) — a scope limitation the survey should note when this study's eventual results, if published, are compared against domain-level studies.
- **What makes two agent reviewers independent:** this is the most direct evidence yet collected in this survey on that question, but it answers it only for a human-in-the-loop LLM setup, not for autonomous agents: independence here is stipulated as two different human operators separately using ChatGPT, with no protocol detail on model-level independence controls (sampling seed, context isolation, or whether "duplicate" runs use fresh sessions). This is a data point that the field currently defines inter-rater independence procedurally (different people) rather than architecturally (different agents/contexts), consistent with the survey's observation that no deep-read work so far define agent-level independence.

## Evidence limits
- **No empirical results exist in this work.** Every number above is a planned sample size, planned power, or planned analysis — not a finding. Any future citation of this work for an actual agreement/kappa figure would be an error; the eventual results (if published) would appear in a separate paper this survey does not yet have.

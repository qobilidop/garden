# Responsible Integration of Artificial Intelligence in Rapid Reviews: A Position Statement From the Cochrane Rapid Reviews Methods Group

## Evidence

- Commentary/position statement authored by the Cochrane Rapid Reviews
  Methods Group (all 8 authors are group co-convenors; Gartlehner and
  Kamel also co-convene the separate Cochrane AI Methods Group).
  Complements a companion 2025 joint position statement from Cochrane,
  Campbell Collaboration, JBI, and the Collaboration for Environmental
  Evidence (Flemyng et al., ref [3]), and endorses RAISE (Responsible AI
  use in Systematic Evidence Synthesis, ref [23]) as its foundation.
- Motivating context: a preprint (Cao et al., medRxiv 2025, ref [2])
  reported an LLM-powered tool autonomously reproducing and updating 12
  Cochrane reviews in 2 days — cited as the trigger for this statement.
- Textbox 1's recommendations, condensed: (1) never fully automate any
  review step — human oversight required; (2) use AI for quality
  assurance complementing human judgment, esp. mitigating
  single-reviewer workflow risk (flag missed studies, inconsistencies,
  extraction errors); (3) be transparent — specify tools/tasks/
  verification in the protocol, document in the "AI Use Disclosure"
  section, record model version and prompts; (4) demonstrate the tool
  upholds methodological rigor; (5) authors remain fully accountable
  regardless of AI use; (6) respect copyright/licensing; (7) adhere to
  RAISE and the joint Cochrane position statement.
- AI uses sanctioned: secondary/QA-check role — e.g. re-examining
  abstracts excluded under single-reviewer screening (Cochrane guidance
  permits single-reviewer screening when dual-screening agreement is
  high [16,17], which a cited crowd-based RCT found "will likely miss
  some eligible studies" [18]); AI as secondary data-extraction
  reviewer, against evidence that up to 50% of human-extracted data
  elements contain errors [19,20].
- AI uses cautioned/prohibited: full automation of any step or the
  whole review; AI as sole/primary decision-maker without human
  verification; omitting model-version/prompt documentation; AI as
  author ("AI tools cannot be listed as authors, nor can they be
  accountable for their errors"). Risks named: incorrect responses,
  fabricated data/references, bias, misinformation.
- Effectiveness evidence cited (secondhand, from Clark et al., Research
  Synthesis Methods 2025, ref [22]): AI screening incorrect-inclusion
  rates 0%–29% (median 10%); AI data-extraction error rates 4%–31%
  (median 14%).
- Forward-looking caution (Sec. 5): predicts more "fully automated
  outputs — reviews that appear well-written and methodologically sound
  on the surface but lack the depth of critical evaluation," placing
  vigilance burden on peer reviewers/editors. Position to be reviewed
  "at least biannually."
- No original empirical data (Data Availability: "not applicable") —
  purely a normative/guidance document synthesizing others' findings.

## Bearing on RQs

RQ3 primarily: a norms-setting document from a named methods-group
authority (not an individual-study finding), giving an explicit,
enumerated principle set for what AI use is sanctioned vs. prohibited
across rapid-review stages, and naming the concrete disclosure
mechanism ("AI Use Disclosure" section, protocol pre-registration of AI
intent). Also bears on RQ4: the sanctioned "AI as secondary
reviewer/quality-assurance" pattern is explicitly framed as an
independence/verification design — AI re-checks human single-reviewer
exclusions rather than replacing dual human review — directly relevant
to independence-of-verification questions.

## Evidence limits

All quantitative figures (0%–29%/median 10% screening error; 4%–31%/
median 14% extraction error; ~50% human extraction error rate; 12
reviews in 2 days; "almost 100 studies" evidence map) are the authors'
citations of other primary sources, not original findings of this
commentary — treat as secondhand pointers requiring the cited source
for verification, not as this paper's own measurements.

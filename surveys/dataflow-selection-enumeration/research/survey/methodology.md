# Mapping methodology and validity record

The survey is a systematic map rather than a statistical literature review. It
classifies semantic objects, algorithms, omission mechanisms, guarantees, and
assumptions across neighboring research communities. The design follows
systematic-mapping guidance, separate backward and forward snowballing, and
auditable reporting of secondary studies.

## Implemented controls

- Research questions, selection rules, extraction dimensions, and stable
  exclusion codes are versioned in `protocol.md`.
- Audited searches retain exact queries or seeds, counts, dispositions, and
  frozen result sets; non-replayable early discovery is kept separate.
- Search indexes are discovery instruments. Technical extraction uses primary
  sources and records page, section, definition, theorem, or algorithm anchors.
- Every critical work is deep-read and receives separate backward and forward
  citation chases. Defective index bibliographies are supplemented from the
  primary work.
- Active synthesis claims and manuscript citation/section pairs are checked
  against anchored claim-evidence rows.
- Recurring update searches have a versioned registry and independently
  tracked completion state. Fetching does not count as completion until its
  effects are reconciled through the manuscript.

The initial campaign's counts and closure outcome are frozen in `baseline.md`.
Current counts and overdue work are generated by `scripts/survey/update.py
status` rather than duplicated in this document.

## Remaining threats

**Selection reliability.** Initial extraction used one primary agent. Later
rounds were duplicate-screened by separate agent passes, but these were not
independent human review teams and no inter-rater calibration statistic was
computed. Human review remains necessary before conventional publication.

**Index coverage and metadata quality.** Citation indexes omit bibliographies,
leave identifiers unresolved, conflate versions, and sometimes attach incorrect
DOIs. Frozen snapshots expose these defects but cannot repair all missing
records. Primary publisher or author sources control technical claims.

**Vocabulary drift.** The map crosses symbolic execution, term graphs,
hardware, demand semantics, event structures, decision diagrams, geometric
enumeration, and model enumeration. Registered searches and heterogeneous
snowballing reduce but cannot eliminate vocabulary-dependent recall failures.

**Temporal validity.** Freshness is relative to the registered sources,
cadences, and most recent reconciled execution. A newly published work can make
the manuscript stale before the next scheduled cycle.

**Synthesis bias.** The formal framework is an interpretive bridge. Every
equivalence must distinguish a theorem proved in a cited work from a derivation
obtained after changing the observer or adding instrumentation.

**API reproducibility.** Search rankings and metadata change. Committed
snapshots reproduce what was screened, while exact query text and execution
dates permit methodological audit; they do not guarantee that an API rerun will
return the same ordering.

# ray-shooting reverse search for central arrangements

## Evidence

### Why it matters

Ferrez, Fukuda, and Liebling give the pre-Rada ray-shooting reverse-search
algorithm for cells of a central hyperplane arrangement. Theorem 4.1 improves
the stated general Avis--Fukuda bound by a factor of the dimension and gives
the same \(O(n\,LP(n,d)\,|C|)\) time and \(O(nd)\) working-space form that Rada and
Černý later call modified reverse search (`m-RS`). It therefore closes a
priority gap in our account of exact output-polynomial affine sign
enumeration.

The paper's main application reduces fixed-rank convex binary quadratic
maximization to enumeration of zonotope vertices. That application is
substantive, but the arrangement-enumeration section has its own theorem,
algorithm, implementation, and arithmetic study.

### Optimization and geometric reductions

For a positive-semidefinite \(Q=V^TV\) of fixed rank \(d\), the paper rewrites

\[
  \max_{x\in\{0,1\}^n}x^TQx
  =\max_{z\in Z(V)}\lVert z\rVert^2,
\]

where \(Z(V)=V[0,1]^n\) is the zonotope generated by the columns of \(V\).
Convexity implies that a maximum occurs at a zonotope vertex. Theorem 3.1
recalls the \(O(n^{d-1})\) fixed-dimensional vertex bound, and Theorem 3.2
credits an asymptotically optimal \(O(n^{d-1})\) incremental generator. That
algorithm stores the full output and face incidences, using output-size memory,
and is described as difficult to implement.

The normal fan gives an order-reversing bijection between faces of (Z(V))
and faces of the central arrangement

\[
  \mathcal A(V)=\{y:v_j^Ty=0\}_{j=1}^n.
\]

In particular, zonotope vertices correspond one-to-one with full-dimensional
strict sign cells \(C(V)\subseteq\{-,+\}^n\). The enumerated record is a dense
total sign vector, from which the associated zonotope vertex is the sum of the
positively marked generators.

### Ray-shooting reverse search

After removing zero columns and coalescing collinear columns, adjacency is a
single feasible sign flip. One LP decides whether index \(j\) is flippable.
Avis--Fukuda's minimum-index parent can require up to \(n\) LPs per parent
evaluation. Ferrez et al. instead fix an interior point \(p^\star\) of the root
cell and a canonical interior point \(p\) of the current cell, shoot the segment
toward \(p^\star\), and flip the first separating hyperplane hit. This computes
the parent with one LP-sized interior-point calculation plus ray shooting.

The finite parent map induces a unique rooted spanning tree. Standard reverse
search traverses that tree depth first without a visited set. Theorem 4.1 gives

\[
  O\!\left(n\,LP(n,d)\,|C|\right)
\]

time and \(O(nd)\) working space for a rational \(d\times n\) matrix. Under
the paper's LP-cost abstraction, this is output polynomial and visits each
tree vertex once.

Central symmetry lets the implementation enumerate only cells whose last sign
is positive by intersecting with a translated copy of the last hyperplane; the
other half is obtained by sign negation. This optimization needs to be included
when interpreting the theorem's output contract.

### Literal pseudocode and tie qualifications

The displayed `CellEnumeration` procedure traverses the positive-last-sign
tree but contains no explicit `output(c)` statement. Taken literally, it emits
nothing and does not reconstruct the antipodal half. The surrounding text and
theorem make the intended enumeration clear: emit a cell on its first visit,
then output its sign negation or otherwise reconstruct the symmetric half.
The theorem should be credited with those standard but elided steps, not as a
verified implementation of the literal listing.

The ray-shooting parent also requires a uniquely selected interior point and a
deterministic rule when several hyperplanes are hit simultaneously. The paper
proposes solving a bounded interior LP with a deterministic algorithm and says
to use standard symbolic perturbation for ray ties. It does not formalize the
lexicographic witness representation or coefficient growth. These are
precision qualifications rather than evidence against the reverse-search
tree theorem.

### Parallel and arithmetic evidence

The ZRAM implementation distributes reverse-search subtrees. Tables 2--3 show
near-linear speedup on the larger tested instance up to eight processors, but
the paper proves no work/span, communication, or load-balance theorem. The
parallel claim is algorithmic decomposability plus experiments.

Both floating-point and GMP rational modes are evaluated. Floating point
eventually fails on large cases through an explicit inconsistent LP result;
rational arithmetic agrees when floating point succeeds but is 14--27 times
slower on the reported small cases. The theorem is for rational input and an
LP-cost abstraction, not the floating-point implementation and not a
coefficient-bit bound.

- Section 4 and Theorem 4.1: regularity, adjacency, ray-shooting parent, and

  floating-point arithmetic.

  local-search heuristic.

## Bearing on RQs

In the strict-affine all-sites-observed specialization, every arrangement cell
is one dense selection observation and its open input fiber. Ferrez et al.
therefore subsume the generic duplicate-free reverse-search traversal and the
\(O(KQ\,LP(Q,D))\) bound for that case. Rada--Černý's later incremental method
has the same asymptotic LP-relative output bound while using a different
sign-prefix enumeration skeleton.

The paper has no requested root, enabled closure, input-dependent site domain,
typed residual graph, contextual site identity, or boundary-inclusive fiber.
It emits no general program residual. Its contribution is exactly the dense
strict real-affine geometric subproblem, not the sparse graph observer.

## Evidence limits

- The sign output is dense, strict, and full dimensional.
- Literal emission and antipodal reconstruction are elided from the
  pseudocode.
- Canonical interior witnesses and simultaneous ray hits rely on deterministic
  LP and symbolic-perturbation conventions that are not formalized.
- Parallel evidence is empirical, and no bit-complexity or delay theorem is
  given.

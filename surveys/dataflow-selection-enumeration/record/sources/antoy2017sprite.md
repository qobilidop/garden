## Why it matters

Sprite turns the Fair Scheme into a native Curry compiler and states the
partial-map representation even more directly: each work-queue expression owns
a fingerprint mapping choice identifiers to `left`, `right`, or `either`.
Together with a fair computation queue, this is a literal implementation
precedent for exhaustive demand-populated sparse decision records.

It still enumerates program values generated by nondeterministic choices rather
than input fibers induced by deterministic selection sites. The paper provides
implementation and benchmark evidence, not a new theorem for projected or
duplicate-free fingerprint enumeration.

## Operational mechanism

Sprite lowers Curry through FlatCurry and ICurry to an imperative graph-rewrite
runtime implemented with LLVM. Choices are heap nodes with fresh integer
identifiers. Pull-tab steps copy identifiers. A work queue stores all live
expressions; after a bounded amount of work the active expression is moved to
the tail. A root choice forks the current computation into two queue entries,
and a constructor value is yielded to the consumer.

Every queue expression owns a fingerprint mapping identifiers to the three-way
status `{left,right,either}`. Selecting an alternative records the outcome;
later copies with the same identifier must agree. The paper uses fingerprints
only to prevent inconsistent call-time choices, not to present them as user
results or as canonical equivalence classes.

The word *active* appears here for the expression currently at the head of the
work queue. This reinforces why “active selection” would be ambiguous in our
paper: activity is a scheduler state, whereas our property is membership in an
input- and root-relative enabled closure.

## Correctness and completeness boundary

Section 3.3 defines operational completeness informally as eventual production
of every value given enough resources. It attributes the proof of correctness
to the Fair Scheme paper. The implementation paper itself does not state a new
formal theorem, and the cited extended Fair Scheme paper leaves its strong
eventual-production statement as a conjecture.

The queue argument is persuasive as a fairness mechanism, but it does not prove
duplicate-free fingerprint enumeration. Several fingerprints may yield the same
constructor value, and the paper neither outputs fingerprints nor blocks an
already emitted projection.

## Performance evidence

The main evaluation compares Sprite with KiCS2 and other Curry systems. On the
choice-heavy `PermSort` benchmark, Sprite and KiCS2 both fit exponential curves
whose slopes differ by less than two percent; the observed advantage is treated
as a constant-factor implementation effect. No output-sensitive bound is given.

The paper discusses constant-time fresh integer allocation and the possibility
of renumbering live identifiers during garbage collection. These identities are
dynamic and computation-local, unlike static occurrence-path names stable
across symbolic inputs.

## Relationship to selection observations

Sprite directly subsumes the data-structure claim “a sparse partial map from
stable copied-choice IDs to outcomes, grown as demanded.” It does not supply:

- deterministic guard predicates over symbolic inputs;
- exact inverse images of one observation;
- disjoint or duplicate-free observation enumeration;
- multi-way static graph selectors without auxiliary binary IDs;
- context-qualified call-site identity; or
- compositional symbolic summaries.

## Questions and weaknesses

- The implementation paper relies on rather than strengthens the Fair Scheme
  proof.
- The ternary `either` is an implementation totalization of an absent map entry;
  it is not a proof that unobserved choices form exact input don't-cares.
- No bound is given in terms of distinct fingerprints or emitted values.
- Fingerprint equality is not a quotient over equal results.

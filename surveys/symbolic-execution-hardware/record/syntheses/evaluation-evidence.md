# Evaluation evidence

## Current conclusion

The 17 deep reads demonstrate functioning implementations and reported positive
outcomes on their studied configurations. They do not establish implementation
correctness, general effectiveness, or a quantitative tool ranking. Designs,
horizons, coverage denominators, setup effort, baselines, semantic bridges, and
completion policies are too heterogeneous for an effect-size synthesis.

## Support and limits

Early work largely supplies feasibility demonstrations; later directed and
hybrid systems add comparisons but retain different targets and bounds.
Several security deep reads provide replayable positive results, while negative
outcomes remain model- and budget-qualified. Among the deep reads, Rudkowski
reports the widest combination observed here of variants, peripherals, mutants,
ablations, prior-tool comparison, and larger-limit cases; that is not a claim
about every include. Preprints and agent adjudication are part of the corpus and
must travel with headline counts.

The catalog does not code output contract, replay status, proof status, author
group, or toolchain across all 31 records. Frequency claims about “most” results,
“few” proofs, or concentration therefore cannot be made corpus-wide. Those
observations may be scoped only to the deep reads unless new facets are audited.

## Manuscript consequence

Open the evidence section with feasibility rather than “the methods work.”
Scope superlatives to the 17 deep reads and name the compared dimensions. Avoid
field-size, maturity, and frequency inferences from the non-closed map.
